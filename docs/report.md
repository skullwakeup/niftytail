# Extreme downside risk in the Nifty 50: an out-of-sample comparison of EVT and GARCH-EVT forecasts

**NIFTYTAIL report (Day 14-4, D61 (h); corrected Day 15C, D65).** Claim policy D49 T6: the headline is T1 of `docs/results_review.md`, which holds in all three passes; every other result, including the main finding H on the price index, names its passes and is labelled *pass-dependent* (its verdict differs across the three test passes) and/or *borderline* (decided by a p-value in [0.01, 0.10]). Results are quoted or closely paraphrased from `docs/results_review.md` (owner-approved, D46 and D49), with that file as their source; table values are copied from the tracked JSON named under each table. No per-day value or date appears except the first and last training dates.

## Abstract

We forecast the one-day Value-at-Risk (VaR) and Expected Shortfall (ES) of losses on the Nifty 50 index and backtest six models on 5,716 out-of-sample trading days with a rolling 1,000-day window: a Gaussian benchmark, historical simulation (HS), a peaks-over-threshold generalized Pareto model (POT-GPD), and three models on the standardized residuals of one AR(1)-GJR filter (GARCH-N, filtered historical simulation FHS, and GARCH-EVT). The threshold of the tail models was chosen on the training data only, by a procedure whose behaviour was first measured in a simulation study, and was committed before any out-of-sample forecast was computed. The frozen plan excluded GARCH; the three conditional models were added by amendment (D38, D39), also before any out-of-sample forecast was computed. The evaluation uses three passes: the price index with a 1,000-day window (primary), the same with a 2,000-day window, and the total-return index with a 1,000-day window. In all three passes the unconditional models show clustered VaR breaches (Christoffersen independence rejected) while the conditional models do not; GARCH-EVT has a lower FZ0 joint VaR-ES score than POT-GPD at both primary levels; POT-GPD and HS are never distinguished; the Gaussian model shows the fat-tail pattern; and GARCH-N is rejected by the Kupiec and ES tests at 99%. On the price index, only FHS and GARCH-EVT pass every coverage test at both primary levels in both windows; this statement is pass-dependent, because on the total-return index GARCH-EVT's 99% ES test rejects, and borderline. The primary pass reproduces byte-for-byte on the development machine.

## 1 Question and contribution

The question is whether extreme value theory (EVT) improves the forecasting of extreme one-day losses of the Nifty 50, once the volatility clustering of daily returns is taken into account. EVT models the tail of the loss distribution above a high threshold by a generalized Pareto distribution [L01, L02, L05]; GARCH-EVT applies the same tail model to the residuals of a volatility filter [L15]. Prior work applies EVT, GARCH and their combination to the Indian market [L32, L33].

The contribution is methodological discipline rather than a new model:

- **What was fixed in advance, and what was amended.** The plan (`docs/plan_v2.2.md`, frozen by sha256) fixed three unconditional models, the tests, the levels and the decision rules, and it excluded GARCH ("No GARCH in v1", D9). The three conditional models were added by amendment (D38, D39) after the training analysis (D36, D37), the Day-3 full-sample audit (D25-D27) and the Day-4 full-sample EDA (D29 (8)), and before any out-of-sample forecast was computed. D38 (2) cited a full-sample statistic, which D39 (b) later withdrew. The threshold was selected on the training sample only and committed (D37) before any out-of-sample forecast was computed. The other departures from the plan's own rules (D33, D36, D37 (b), D39 (a)) are stated in section 3 and listed in section 7.
- **A simulation-tested threshold procedure.** Before the real selection, the procedure was run on 10 simulated cells with known tails (plan A3), so its tendencies were known before it was used; its false-selection trigger fired (D36).
- **Joint VaR-ES evaluation.** Besides coverage tests, models are compared with the FZ0 scoring function, for which (VaR, ES) is jointly elicitable [L26-L28], and the Diebold-Mariano test [L30], with a Holm correction where the plan declared a family [L37].
- **A claim policy.** Only statements that hold in all three passes are headlined (D49 T6).
- **Reproducibility.** Every published artefact is regenerated byte-for-byte from the code on the development machine (section 8; `docs/reproducibility.md`).

## 2 Data

For 10 randomly drawn PR dates, the comparison with public reference closes was run by the implementer, on closes supplied by the reviewer from secondary sources; the owner did not perform it. 7 dates matched, and 3 are unchecked because no independent source was found (`docs/data.md` §8). For TRI, the owner's spot check found that 10 of 10 drawn dates match niftyindices.com (`docs/data.md` §9).

## 3 Methodology

The full specification is `docs/methodology.md`; the decisions are in `docs/decisions.md`.

**Losses and the rolling design.** The daily loss is the negative log return, L_t = -ln(P_t / P_{t-1}). For each out-of-sample day the six models are fitted to the previous W losses (W = 1000 in the primary design, 2000 as a sensitivity; `[design]` in `configs/base.toml`) and forecast the next day's loss. Nothing carries over from one day to the next, and a day's forecast never sees that day or a later one (D41). The levels are c = 0.95 and 0.99 (primary) and 0.995 and 0.999 (descriptive; `[levels]` in `configs/base.toml`).

**The six models** (D40):

- **Gaussian:** mean plus standard deviation times the normal quantile; ES from the normal density.
- **HS:** VaR is the empirical quantile L_(⌈Wc⌉) of the window, and ES is the exact average of the losses beyond it (plan A1).
- **POT-GPD:** a generalized Pareto distribution fitted by maximum likelihood to the excesses over u = L_(⌈Wq⌉) with q = q_selected = 0.88. VaR and ES follow from the fitted tail [L15] (methodology §6).
- **Conditional models** (added by amendment D38, D39; the frozen plan excluded GARCH): one AR(1) mean with GJR(1,1) variance [L39], fitted by Gaussian quasi-maximum likelihood (filter F4, chosen by training BIC, D39) to each window, gives the next day's μ and σ and standardized residuals z. Then:
  - **GARCH-N** is μ + σ times the normal VaR and ES;
  - **FHS** applies HS to z [L35];
  - **GARCH-EVT** applies POT-GPD to z at q_c = 0.88 [L15].

  All three rescale by μ + σ(·).

**Threshold selection (training data only).** The threshold quantile was chosen once, on the training sample. The candidates are q = 0.80, 0.81, …, 0.95. Each is checked for goodness of fit (Anderson-Darling p-values) and combined by the ForwardStop rule [L13, L14], subject to a stable-region condition on the shape estimate and a minimum number of excesses N_min (`docs/threshold_selection.md` §1, D32-D36). The N_min simulation rule failed, and N_min = 100 is an explicit override, a judgement and not a rule outcome (D33; `reports/threshold_selection/nmin_study.json`: `rule.n_min` null, `adopted.source` "D33"). Before the real selection, the plan A3 simulation study measured how the procedure behaves on dependent data with known tails. Its false-selection trigger fired in both cells that carry it (false-selection rates 0.915 and 0.965; `reports/threshold_selection/simstudy/metrics.json`: `triggers[*].false_selection`): the procedure selects below the true threshold, with a risk-understating bias, and the owner decided to proceed as specified (D36 (a)(1), (a)(3)). The real selection gave q* = 0.88, signed off by the owner and committed in `configs/base.toml` before any out-of-sample forecast was computed (D37). The split-half check raised a flag (one half gave no stable region, the other q = 0.80); the flag was accepted and not acted on (D37 (b); `reports/threshold_selection/real/selection.json`: `selection.flags`, `split_half`). At q* the 95% profile-likelihood interval of the shape ξ includes 0, so the training data do not establish a heavy tail at 95%: ξ̂ = 0.115689 with CI [-0.00920515, 0.275268] (`reports/threshold_selection/real/selection.json`, as copied in `docs/xi_path.md`). On the training residuals of the D38 filter the threshold procedure rejected all 16 candidates (rule failure `all_rejected`; `reports/threshold_selection/real_residuals/selection.json`: `selection`), so the GARCH-EVT residual threshold was not data-selected: q_c was set equal to q_selected = 0.88 by design (D39 (a)). Three neighbouring thresholds (0.86, 0.90, 0.91) are carried as declared sensitivities.

**Backtests** (D42; methodology §10-§12). A breach is a day whose loss exceeds the forecast VaR.

- **Kupiec:** the proportion-of-failures likelihood-ratio test [L22].
- **Christoffersen:** the independence (IND) and conditional-coverage (CC) tests [L23], with Monte Carlo p-values (B = 9999, so p ≤ 1e-4 is the floor).
- **ES residual:** the one-sided exceedance-residual test, with a bootstrap p-value [L15] (D14).
- **Basel traffic light:** zones per 250-day block at 99%; 22 full blocks are zoned and the partial block is not (plan A4) [L25].
- **Diebold-Mariano:** the test [L30] on the FZ0 score [L28], computed on a common mask of days on which all compared forecasts are admissible (plan A2). It runs in three families: (i) the unconditional pairs, unadjusted; (ii) the conditional pairs, Holm-adjusted [L37]; (iii) the threshold sensitivities, descriptive only.

All tests are at 5%.

## 5 Robustness

**Window W = 2000 (PR).** The headline T1 holds in this pass too. The window changes three results: HS and POT-GPD reject Kupiec at 0.95 (F6, PR W2000 only); GARCH-EVT no longer beats GARCH-N on FZ0 (F2, *pass-dependent, borderline*); and GARCH-EVT beats FHS at 0.95 (T4, *pass-dependent, borderline*).

**Total-return index (TRI W1000).** On the identical 5,716 days, the TRI and PR passes agree closely: |breaches_TRI - breaches_PR| ≤ 2 for every key and primary level, the traffic-light zone counts are identical for all six keys, and the DM family (ii) verdicts are identical in 5 of 6 (T2, computed from the JSON in `docs/results_review.md`). The one coverage difference that matters is GARCH-EVT's 99% ES test, which rejects on TRI (p 0.0438) and not on PR (p 0.0582) (T3, *pass-dependent, borderline*).

**Threshold sensitivities.** For GARCH-EVT, the threshold barely matters. Its 0.99 breaches are 62 at every q_c key on PR W1000 and 54-55 on PR W2000. None of the 12 GARCH-EVT sensitivity DM tests on PR is significant at 5% (F5). On TRI, 1 of 6 is significant: 0.99 @0.91, p 0.0482307, d_bar +0.00162994, which favours q_c (T5, *pass-dependent, borderline*). For POT-GPD, four sensitivity DM rejections on PR all favour the higher threshold. They are W1000 0.95 @0.9 (p 0.000742831) and @0.91 (p 0.000471212), W1000 0.99 @0.91 (p 0.00527062), and W2000 0.95 @0.86 (p 0.00258428). Their |d_bar| ≤ 0.0052 against |mean FZ0| ≥ 2.88532, which is economically negligible, and q_selected is unchanged (C3). The TRI rejections (0.95 @0.9 p 0.000808242, 0.95 @0.91 p 0.000160991, 0.99 @0.91 p 0.0365789; the last is borderline) also favour the higher q (T5). These rejections are *pass-dependent*: the rejecting cells differ between passes. See [F5](../reports/figures/sensitivity.svg): mean FZ0 difference from q_ref and breach rate against the threshold q for POT-GPD and GARCH-EVT per level and pass; q_selected / q_c marked.

**Out-of-sample blocks (D45, post hoc).** The threshold procedure was rerun on blocks of the out-of-sample losses (raw) and of the filter residuals (descriptive only). In none of the six block entries did the selected threshold change VaR99 or ES99 by more than the pre-declared tolerance: `risk_material` is false in all six (F5; `reports/threshold_selection/oos_blocks/selection.json`). The raw blocks select q below q_selected (0.8, 0.81, 0.85), consistent with the A3 finding (L4). See [F6](../reports/figures/oos_blocks.svg): D45 block q* (raw, residuals) against q_ref, and VaR99 / ES99 relative change with the tolerance bands.

**The rolling shape estimate (D47, descriptive).** Over the 5,716 out-of-sample windows of PR W1000, the median ξ̂ is 0.0978785 for POT-GPD and 0.00895425 for GARCH-EVT. The 95% profile interval contains 0 in a share 0.661477 of the windows for POT-GPD and 0.983905 for GARCH-EVT (`reports/oos/xi_path/summary.json`, as published in `docs/xi_path.md`). Overlapping windows make the path strongly autocorrelated, so these shares are not independent evidence. See [F7](../reports/figures/xi_blocks.svg): per-block median xi and share of profile CIs with lower bound > 0, POT-GPD vs GARCH-EVT (D47); POT-GPD training xi and CI as a band.

## 6 Application (local only)

The same models, configuration and code path are applied to the newest data to produce next-day VaR and ES (D58). The command `forecast` reuses the one per-target function of the backtest (`rolling.forecast_target`) and writes `reports/live/<key>/forecast.json` and `series.json`, both gitignored. The key is a hash of the config, the data manifests and the commit. `forecast --check-run` recomputes 20 randomly drawn past days of the published PR W1000 run from scratch and compares them with the published forecasts; on the development machine the result is 20/20 identical (`docs/reproducibility.md`).

A Streamlit dashboard (D55, D59) shows four sections. *Track record*, *How it works* and *Proof* use only the hash-checked tracked artefacts. *Today* shows the market from the owner's files, the next-day VaR and ES in rupees for a chosen position, a crash calculator (exceedance probabilities and return periods from the fitted tail) and a volatility-shock scenario. Every number on screen is tracked JSON, `forecast` output, the owner's data, or closed-form arithmetic on those. Today never names a model as best.

The daily update (D60) takes end-of-day rows from EODHD, a licensed data vendor, because niftyindices.com and nseindia.com forbid automated collection. A row is accepted only if the overlap with the existing data has identical session dates and identical closes to 2 decimals. `scripts\update.cmd` runs validation, the fetch, the forecast and the check-run, and a scheduled task can run it daily. Today, the day-by-day figures, every live file and every vendor row are local only. Public mode (NIFTYTAIL_PUBLIC=1) removes them and never reads them (D23, D55, D60).

## 7 Limitations

**Risks recorded by the plan (§8):**

1. EVT is close to HS at 99% by construction, so the far-tail advantage is not backtestable.
2. Kupiec over-rejects under clustering.
3. The tests have low power: Kupiec detects a 1.25% true breach rate only 45% of the time (exact power 0.4508 at T = 5,650; methodology §10.3).
4. ForwardStop's false-discovery guarantee is not formally valid for nested thresholds.
5. The stability tolerances are practical, pre-registered constants; N_min = 100 was set by override (D33).
6. The training regime may not represent the later tail shape; this is monitored post hoc and never retuned.
7. The licensing is unclear and could block publication.
8. Circuit-breaker censoring and the holiday/missing-day ambiguity were left to the Day-3 audit (`docs/data.md` §5).
9. Several foundational literature claims are status F (full text not read; `docs/literature.md`).

**Limitations found in the results** (`docs/results_review.md`):

- **L1** *(borderline).* ES is mildly understated: z_bar > 0 for every key at both levels in both windows. GARCH-EVT's W1000 0.99 ES p = 0.0582 and GARCH-EVT@0.86's p = 0.0465, in the direction predicted before the out-of-sample data (D36 (a)(3)). T3 extends this to TRI, where the test crosses 5%.
- **L2** *(pass-dependent, borderline).* POT-GPD at 0.999 has 12 breaches on PR W1000 (binomial p 0.0287412; HS identical) and 9 on PR W2000 (p 0.249519).
- **L3.** Breach clustering in the unconditional models was expected (D38 (7)).
- **L4.** The C3 direction is consistent with the A3 finding and the D45 blocks: the threshold procedure tends to select below the true threshold. This is a limitation for the unconditional model only, since no GARCH-EVT sensitivity test is significant on PR (F5).

The carried limitations of D36, D37, D39 and D45 are listed in `docs/results_review.md`.

**Amendments and departures from the plan's own rules:**

- **Conditional models (D38, D39).** The frozen plan excluded GARCH (D9). GARCH-N, FHS and GARCH-EVT, which carry the headline and the main finding, were added by amendment after the training analysis, the Day-3 full-sample audit and the Day-4 full-sample EDA, and before any out-of-sample forecast was computed. The out-of-sample period was therefore not unseen when they were designed: D38 (2) cited a full-sample statistic, which D39 (b) withdrew.
- **N_min (D33).** The simulation rule failed; N_min = 100 is an override.
- **A3 trigger (D36).** The false-selection trigger fired; the procedure was used unchanged.
- **Split-half flag (D37 (b)).** The flag was raised, accepted and not acted on.
- **Residual threshold (D39 (a)).** The threshold procedure rejected all 16 candidates on the training residuals of the D38 filter; q_c was set equal to q_selected by design.

**Limitations of the tests and of the application:**

- **Independence test.** The Christoffersen IND test looks at lag-1 dependence of breaches only and has low power at a 1% breach rate.
- **Diebold-Mariano.** Its validity for models that are re-estimated every day rests on the fixed length of the rolling window (the argument of Giacomini and White; that paper is not in `docs/literature.md` and was not read).
- **ES test.** The ES residual test is a variant of the McNeil-Frey test [L15], not their test: the residuals are scaled by the ES forecast, and the p-value comes from an i.i.d. bootstrap (methodology §10.7).
- **Filter.** The GJR variance restricts γ ≥ 0 (D39 (c)).
- **Multiplicity.** The coverage tests are not adjusted for multiple testing; only DM family (ii) is (Holm).
- **Dashboard return periods.** They assume independent exceedances and, for GARCH-EVT, are conditional on today's volatility.
- **Special sessions.** The 42 weekend-dated sessions are treated as ordinary trading days (D27; `docs/data.md` §5.1).

**Scope:**

- **One index:** only the Nifty 50, so the results need not transfer to other indices or to single stocks.
- **One horizon:** one day only, so there is no multi-day or scaling result.
- **One window per pass:** W2000 on TRI was not run (insufficient history).
- **One machine:** byte identity was established on one Windows machine only; no second machine was tested, and the tolerance for other machines is documented but unverified (section 8).
- **Licensing:** publication of this report, the code and the aggregate results requires the D23 steps.

## 8 Reproducibility

Every out-of-sample pass, figure and table regenerates byte-for-byte on the development machine (Windows 11, AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D, Python 3.13.17, numpy 2.5.4, scipy 1.18.1, BLAS threads pinned to 1). From commit `c1d506b` on a clean tree, each published artefact was verified at the commit that generated it (`docs/reproducibility.md`):

- **Runs:** `run --verify` for PR W1000, PR W2000 and TRI W1000 took 89, 94 and 85 seconds with 31 workers. All four output files of each pass matched, including `forecasts.csv`, the plan's acceptance criterion (plan §7 row 14).
